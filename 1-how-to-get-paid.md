# How to Get Paid in the Agentic AI Era
*A beginner-friendly guide to the Vendor-Neutral Vertical FDE roadmap*
Source: [The AI Agent Factory](agentfactory.panaversity.org/docs/how-to-get-paid-agentic-ai-era)

---

## 1. The problem (start here)

Two things are happening at once:

- **Developers are losing routine work.** AI now writes a lot of simple code, so easy/junior level jobs are drying up.
- **Companies are wasting money on AI.** About 95% of company AI projects show no real payoff  not because the AI is bad, but because nobody fits it into the company's actual rules, data, and approval steps.

> There's a gap between "AI can technically do this" and "this company is actually using it well." Someone has to close that gap. That's a job needed in today's market.

---

## 2. The market's answered: the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)

An FDE doesn't build a product at head office. They go **inside** a client's company and build there, on real data, until it actually works.

- Demand is exploding: job postings up 800%+ in nine months.
- Big companies (AWS, Microsoft, OpenAI) are spending billions hiring for this.

**But there are two catches (the "two locked gates"):**

1. **Vendor lock-in.** A vendor's FDE only builds on that vendor's platform. The client gets stuck... like a plumber who only installs one brand of pipe.
2. **A closed hiring door.** Usually needs 5+ years experience and a US/European address. Most of the world can't get in.

---

## 3. Our answer: the vendor-neutral vertical FDE

Remove the vendor, and both gates open but now you need something to specialize in.

- **Vendor-neutral** = you're not tied to one AI company's tools. You use whatever works best for the client, so they're never trapped.
- **Vertical** = one profession, in one country. Example: "tax filing in Pakistan" is a vertical. "Accounting" in general is not too broad.

You carry **two things** through the client's door:
1. **The shared method** — the general approach this book teaches (same for everyone).
2. **Your own governed vertical** — one profession's rules and judgment, written down with help from a real expert in that field.

---

## 4. The nine-station roadmap

| # | Station | What you do | What you leave with |
|---|---------|-------------|----------------------|
| 1 | Understand the roles | Read where the FDE role fits | Clarity on which seat fits you |
| 2 | Understand the ecosystem | See the method in action | A working example to copy |
| 3 | Understand the business model | Learn how money flows | The earning map |
| 4 | Understand what you can own | Learn *why* a vertical is the one real asset | The "build first, sell second" rule |
| 5 | Choose your vertical | Pick one profession + find a real expert | A validated vertical (or an honest "not yet") |
| 6 | Design the vertical's knowledge base | Work with your expert to map the rules | A designed first slice of the profession |
| 7 | Build it | Turn the design into a working system | A live, working slice |
| 8 | Connect it to real company data | Make it usable inside a real client | A reusable connection pattern |
| 9 | Go to work | Sell it | Income |

**Stations 1–4 = reading/deciding. 
/
5–8 = building. 
9 = selling.**

Don't skip any stations because each one feeds the next.

---

## 5. Three doors to get paid

| Door | What it looks like | Pay range (published) |
|------|--------------------|-----------------------|
| **The job** | Get hired (by a firm or a client who's seen your work) | ~$170k–$300k+/year (mostly US/Europe, senior) |
| **Freelance/retainer** | Work with several clients, no borders | $2k–$15k+ per project; $4k–$10k/month retainers |
| **Your own startup** | Co-own a business with your expert | No ceiling cause it's a business, not a salary |

Most people go through all three **in order**: freelance work funds the learning → a proven project gets you hired or trusted → the growing vertical becomes a real business.

---

## 6. Earn *while* you're learning: the "service ladder"

You don't need your own vertical to start earning. You can:
- Build a knowledge system from a **client's own** manuals and procedures.
- Build AI "workers" for clients using only general-purpose tools.

This needs no expert and no vertical of your own — it pays within weeks, and it's where almost everyone should start.

---

## 7. How to price the work: sell a result, not your hours

Before building anything, agree **in writing** on three numbers with the client:
1. **Baseline** — what the work costs them today.
2. **Target** — what counts as success.
3. **Acceptance criteria** — how success will be checked.

This written agreement is called a **contract of success**. 

---

## 8. Eight common mistakes (and how to avoid them)

1. **Polishing your résumé instead of building something real.** Fix: build the slice first  it's your proof.
2. **Starting a vertical without a real, committed expert.** Fix: don't launch until someone with real experience has actually signed on.
3. **Picking the biggest market instead of the easiest first sale.** Fix: start small and winnable.
4. **Shipping a too-clean demo.** Fix: test it against messy, real edge cases.
5. **Accepting disguised staff-renting** ("we're just understaffed"). Fix: ask who the actual working team will be.
6. **Spreading across too many professions at once.** Fix: stay narrow one profession, one country.
7. **Forcing a vertical before finding a real expert.** Fix: earn on the service ladder while you keep looking.
8. **Building the "connect to company data" layer too early.** Fix: only build it once your core slice is live and proven.
